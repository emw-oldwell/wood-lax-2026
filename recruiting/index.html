Rachel — start here

This is a working document, not a report. Three things to do, in this order:

  1. Work the calendar. Every women's lacrosse camp between Aug 15 and Oct 4 that you're eligible for is listed by day. Mark each one Going, Maybe or Skip. The page flags days where you've picked two camps that overlap, and keeps a running cost total at the bottom.

One thing to carry into every camp: ask the staff “are you taking a goalie in 2028?” Not “are you recruiting keepers” — your specific class. That one answer is worth more than the rest of the day.

September 1, 2026

The date the whole calendar points at

Sept 1 of junior year is the first day D1 coaches can contact you and the first day you can take visits. In the class of 2027, girls' commitments went from about 6 in August to 595 in September — roughly ninety percent of a class goes in the first month. August exists so you're a name with a face attached when the phones open. Also: Aug 1–14 is an NCAA recruiting shutdown (no contact of any kind, so nobody will answer email) and Aug 28–Sep 3 is a dead period.

The Academic Index — where Rachel stands

Every Ivy on this board scores recruits on the Academic Index before a coach can support them — a 240-point number built from unweighted GPA and the SAT or ACT. Brown, Harvard, Cornell, Dartmouth, Penn and Princeton all use it, and every one of them requires a test score for her application cycle — test-optional is over. The good news is on the left. The whole remaining job is on the right.

The part that's already done

The index uses her unweighted 3.9 — the 4.3 weighted never enters the formula, though course rigor shows up in the pre-read. At 78 of a possible 80, her GPA is two points from perfect. Her admission math is now almost entirely SAT-driven — which is unusually good news, because a test score can be built in a way a four-year GPA can't.

The one job between now and pre-reads: protect the 3.9 through junior year.

The timeline that matters

  • Junior year (2026–27): first real SAT sitting by spring 2027, so a score exists when it counts.
  • June 2027: junior transcript + scores go to coaches.
  • July 1, 2027: official Ivy pre-reads — admissions reviews her transcript and scores and gives coaches a green / yellow / red.
  • Oct 1, 2027: earliest likely letters; ED application ~Nov 1.

What the numbers mean in practice

  • League floor is ~176 — below it no Ivy coach can support anyone.
  • Coaches' rosters must average close to the student body, so women's lacrosse recruits live in the top bands — low-band slots go to football and hockey.
  • Rules of thumb coaches actually use, either test: SAT 1350 / ACT 29 = in the conversation · 1400 / 31 = Brown-tier safe · 1450+ / 33 = safe everywhere.

West Point & the academies

Different system — a Whole Candidate Score that's 60% academics. Her 3.9 sits right at the West Point appointee average, and an SAT around 1250+ (ACT ~27) makes her a direct-entry academic profile (no prep-school year). Recruited-athlete screening floors are far lower. Academics are not her obstacle there.

NCAA housekeeping

  • Register with the NCAA Eligibility Center this year.
  • Core-course minimum is a 2.3 — a non-issue at 3.9. One audit worth doing: 10 core courses (7 in English/Math/Science) completed before senior year starts.
  • The NCAA itself no longer requires a test. Every Ivy on her list does.

Fine print: the Ivy League has never published the index formula — this uses the standard reconstruction (unweighted GPA × 20, plus SAT ÷ 10) that consulting firms and the Harvard Crimson's reporting converge on. Treat any single number as ±5–10 points, and the pre-read next July as the only answer that counts. Her SAT target saves and syncs like everything else on this page.

Your questions

Four things to work out. Type your answers straight into the boxes — they save into the page's link along with everything else, so use Copy my link when you're done.

1Non-negotiables

Answer these first — each one cuts the board before anything else does.

  1. Is West Point really a yes? Are you interested in the service academies — how do you feel about them?

    Army is in Tier 1, coming off the best season in program history. It also comes with a service commitment after graduation. There's no wrong answer — but a real one changes how much of September gets spent there.

  2. How far from home is too far?

    Tier 1 alone runs from Clemson at 130 miles to Oregon at 2,700. Ask for a number of hours, not a feeling.

  3. Big school or small school?

    Tier 1 spans 4,400 at West Point to 32,200 at Colorado. That is not a detail — it is two different lives.

  4. If lacrosse ended after freshman year, would you still want to be there?

    Ask it about every school before spending a Saturday and $375 at its camp.

2Academics and major

Tier 1 contains schools that admit 3.7% of applicants and schools that admit 88%. That spread is the widest thing on the board.

  1. What do you want to study, or what three things are you closest to sure about?

    Hopkins, Vanderbilt, JMU and Oregon draw completely different maps. This question sorts Tier 1 faster than anything else on this page.

  2. Where do your grades and scores actually sit right now?

    Ivy and academy admissions run on an academic index. Coach support is a boost, not a bypass. Better to know before the Harvard clinic than after.

  3. Do you want a place where lacrosse is the identity, or one thing you do?

    Rutgers and Clemson versus Dartmouth and Brown. Both are good answers; they are not the same answer.

  4. Would you take a top academic school with a losing program over a weaker school winning titles?

    Harvard went 6–9 last season. Make her rank that trade out loud.

3The goalie math

Of the 32 schools here, 23 have already signed a 2027 goalie — including nine now sitting in Tier 1. That does not make them wrong choices; it makes the ask different.

  1. For the nine Tier 1 schools that already took a 2027 keeper, what is the actual pitch?

    Clemson, Colorado, Cornell, Denver, Harvard, JMU, Oregon, St. Joe's and West Point all have theirs. Going to their camps is about being the known name if that keeper decommits, if they take two, or if 2029 opens — all real, none guaranteed. Worth deciding deliberately rather than by momentum.

  2. At every camp, ask one question: are you taking a goalie in 2028?

    Not “are you recruiting keepers.” The specific class. A straight yes or no there is worth more than the whole day, and it is the fastest way to re-sort these tiers with real information instead of inference.

  3. Compete immediately, or develop behind someone for two years?

    Hopkins, Cornell, St. Joe's and West Point have nothing rostered in front of her by 2029. Northwestern carries five keepers and just won a national title.

  4. How much does the goalie coach matter to you?

    Devon Wills runs Harvard. Shea Dolce just took over keepers at Dartmouth. Both are Tier 1. Nobody else on this board offers that level of position coaching.

4Campus and life

The part nobody asks until April of senior year.

  1. City, college town, or campus in the woods?

    Philadelphia and Pittsburgh versus Clemson and Boulder versus Hanover and Ithaca — all Tier 1.

  2. Does weather matter to you?

    Boulder, Eugene, Hanover and Nashville are all in Tier 1.

  3. How often do you want family at games?

    That one answer reshuffles Tier 1 immediately — Clemson at 130 miles reads very differently from Oregon at 2,700.

  4. What should a Saturday in October look like?

    A Rutgers football weekend and a Dartmouth fall are not the same product.

Where this could be wrong. The public commitment databases have a coverage gap from roughly January to early March 2026, and goalie commitments get announced less reliably than attackers'. "No 2027 GK" at Brown, Dartmouth, Vanderbilt and Pitt is a well-supported read, not a certainty; Johns Hopkins, Penn, UNC and Virginia are marked unclear because too few commits surfaced to judge. Notre Dame's Aug 23 start time and Clemson's exact grad-year eligibility are not published. Several official camp pages are stale and were deliberately ignored in favour of the live registration hosts — Maryland's terpslacrosse.com still shows 2024 dates, and Princeton's page is frozen at December 2023. "GK room 2029" counts only keepers currently rostered who would still be there when she arrives; it cannot see 2027-class keepers.

Scholarships. The old 12-scholarship cap in women's lacrosse was replaced under the House settlement by a 38-player roster limit, and opted-in schools may fund more. The Ivy League did not opt in — Brown, Harvard, Princeton, Cornell, Dartmouth, Yale and Penn are need-based aid only. West Point awards no athletic aid at all.
